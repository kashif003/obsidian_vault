# Proof Over Promises (Portfolio & GitHub)

Deploy Real Projects: Don't just build tutorial clones (like a standard to-do app). Build real tools—like an API, a CLI tool, or a web service—and make sure they have a live URL or an easy install command.

Write Excellent READMEs: Hiring managers want to see your decision-making process. Use your README to explain why you chose a specific database (e.g., PostgreSQL over MongoDB) or how you structured your authentication.

Show Consistent History: A GitHub profile with steady commits over several months looks much better than three repositories created the week you started applying.

2. Marketing Yourself (Resume & LinkedIn)

Bypass the Trash Bin: Your resume needs to clearly signal your skills immediately. Focus heavily on the technologies you used in your deployed projects and the specific problems you solved.

Polish Your LinkedIn: Keep your profile updated, professional, and aligned with your resume. It acts as your public-facing signal to recruiters.

3. The Hustle (Applying & Networking)

Targeted Strategy: Treat the job hunt systematically rather than randomly sending out hundreds of identical applications.

Meet Humans: Go to local tech meetups and networking events. Getting your resume directly in front of the right people is often more effective than cold applying.

Consider Relocation: Think through the trade-offs of relocating versus holding out for a remote role, especially for your first junior position.

4. Nailing the Interview

Talk About Trade-offs: In technical interviews, don't just say, "I used this tool because the tutorial did." Explain the real-world problems you ran into and why your solution made sense.

Avoid Common Pitfalls: Practice answering behavioral questions and be prepared to speak clearly about your coding journey and problem-solving strategies.
