How many types of anomalies are there and how can we introduce the anomalies in our use case?


Amomalies in [[Master Thesis/literature review/DAYPSCI]], 

+ T1 stuck@0 and T2 stuck@1: sensor is stuck
+ T3 delay/jiter: The edge arrives little late.
+ T4 noise: electromagnetic interference from motors or drives
+ T5 glitch: short signal pulses of random duration
+ T6 degradation: signal worsening gradually along time.

valve faults:

+ T1 stuck and T2 stuck: the valve doesnt respond to its command
+ T3 delay: The valve is late or reacts a little late


faults form the [[Master Thesis/literature review/DAYPSCI]] we can use:

1. Delay within the timeout (the main one)
	- Command-to-sensor time is longer than usual but still shorter than the PLC timeout. 
	 - Example: normal 0.4 s, timeout 2 s, inject 0.6 to 1.5 s.
2. Jitter
	- Same mean timing, but the variance grows.
	- Invisible to the control logic, visible to a detector.
3. Slow drift / degradation
	- Response time creeps up over many cycles (e.g. 0.40 s, 0.45 s, 0.50 s, ...).
	- Stays under the timeout until you decide it should cross it.
	- Gives you the pre-failure window.
4. Glitches the logic ignores
	- Very short pulses, shorter than the input filter or the scan time.
	- Or pulses on a sensor that is not being read in the current state.
5. Chatter around a real edge
	- A real transition bounces a few times and then settles.
	- The logic sees a normal edge, but the pattern is abnormal.
6. Extra / spurious edges on non-critical or idle signals
	- A sensor that should be quiet flickers briefly.
7. Asymmetric timing
	- Rising edge normal, falling edge slow (or the reverse).
	- Still within tolerance.
8. Pulse-width and dwell-time changes
	- A part-presence sensor stays high slightly longer or shorter than normal.
	- Time between consecutive part arrivals shifts slightly.
9. Cycle-time creep
	- A station takes slightly longer on each part.
	- Operation order stays correct, but the gaps between steps change.
10. Early edges within tolerance
	- A sensor confirms earlier than normal.
	- Odd, but accepted by the logic.
11. Short recoverable dropouts
	- A sensor drops to 0 for a very short window while the logic is not waiting on it, then returns.
